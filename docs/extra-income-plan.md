## How the franchise will be judged (session 2)
Ask for these before anything else. If the franchisor won't give them in writing, that is the answer.
1. **The disclosure document.** In Mexico the franchisor must give a *Circular de Oferta de Franquicia*
   (COF) before signing (the law sets a minimum lead time; check it is respected). In Spain the
   equivalent pre-contract information must arrive at least 20 days before signing. No COF = walk away.
2. **Full cash needed**: franchise fee + build-out + equipment + first stock + deposits + **6–12 months
   of working capital**. Most failures are owners who ran out of cash before break-even.
3. **Unit economics from real owners**, not from the sales pitch. Call at least 5 current franchisees
   (and any who left). Ask: monthly sales, what's left after royalties, rent and staff, how long to
   break even, would you buy it again.
4. **Royalties + marketing fee** taken from *sales*, not profit. 6–10% of sales can wipe out the margin
   of a small food or retail unit.
5. **Contract terms**: length, renewal cost, exit/resale rules, non-compete, forced suppliers,
   mandatory remodels, who chooses the location.
6. **Who runs it.** The user is in Spain about 4 months a year and may change jobs. An absentee
   owner with hired staff usually earns much less than the pitch assumes. Needs a named, trusted
   operator and the numbers must still work after paying them.
7. **Brand track record**: years operating, number of units, how many closed in the last 2–3 years.
   Check the Asociación Mexicana de Franquicias, Google reviews of existing units, and news.

**Hard rule (proposed):** don't fund it with debt, credit cards or a severance payment, and don't
commit more than the user could lose without hurting rent and food. With a job at risk, keep an
emergency fund of 3–6 months of expenses first.
